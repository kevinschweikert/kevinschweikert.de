site.gleam is actually doing three unrelated jobs today: modeling the domain (Site, Page, Body, Meta), discovering content from disk (from_folder, extract_meta), and rendering a page to markup (common, to_elements, render, browser, feed, absolutize, highlight — over half the file, lines 118–288). The book's own "where everything went" table already names the fix: that rendering chunk is supposed to live in view/article.gleam, leaving site.gleam as just the model plus thin page-level queries (rel_path, abs_path, updated_date).

My recommendation: split off everything from common through highlight into view/article.gleam, and move the stray paths stub (which per our earlier conversation shouldn't be here at all) into build.gleam as a private function. The main tradeoff is that view/article.gleam becomes a new one-way dependent on site for the Page/Site types — a cheap, expected cost, not a real downside. The one thing I'd leave for a separate decision: from_folder/extract_meta is disk-reading content discovery, which arguably belongs in content.gleam next to Source/Revision rather than in the domain model — but that's a second, independent cut you don't have to make at the same time.
